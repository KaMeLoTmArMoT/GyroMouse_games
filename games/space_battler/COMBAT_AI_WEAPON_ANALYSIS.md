- **Space Combat AI & Weapon Dynamics — Architectural Analysis and Implementation Plan**
  - Baseline: commit `491f2d8`; source inspected on 2026-11-04. This is an analysis and proposed implementation contract. Numerical balance values are initial experiment settings, not measured playtest outcomes.
  - Scope: the active client simulation assembled by `frontend/src/App.tsx:72–95`. The supplied symptom descriptions are evidence from the playtest; no screenshot was attached to this task, and no browser replay was performed.
  - Source shorthand throughout this report:
    - **S**: [frontend/src/engine/simulation.ts](frontend/src/engine/simulation.ts).
    - **B**: [frontend/src/engine/fleetBuilder.ts](frontend/src/engine/fleetBuilder.ts).
    - **P**: [frontend/src/engine/fleetPresets.ts](frontend/src/engine/fleetPresets.ts).
    - **T**: [frontend/src/types/combat.ts](frontend/src/types/combat.ts).
  - Primary recommendation: correct firing eligibility, damage provenance, and time-step/contact behavior first; separate navigation targets from mount targets; then introduce stable maneuver states and tune ranges/flak against repeatable encounters. Otherwise numerical buffs will conceal unreachable arcs and invalid firing attempts.

- **1. Verified causes and important corrections**
  - **[Targeting] Fighter opening silence — confirmed, with two independent locks.**
    - S:165–166 permits fire only in `attack`. S:347–362 creates every wing and member in `staging`.
    - S:377–425 waits for every healthy member to assemble near its rally slot, the wing launch schedule, the team launch schedule, and all weapons to be nearly reloaded. An obstructed member can indefinitely delay everyone.
    - S:279–285 returns the wing target before evaluating group priority. S:383–393 acquires that target using a hard-coded `capitals` order. Merely adding a nearest-fighter branch below this return will not enable dogfighting.
    - S:297–303 prefers capitals only when they exist, rather than absolutely excluding other classes. Fighter-only encounters still suffer staging and whole-wing single-target behavior.
  - **[Damage] Corvette flak is stronger and more collective than the builder suggests.**
    - B:243–290 defines two 14-damage, 0.15-second, 360-unit flak mounts. S:53–70, 1115–1133, 1239–1253 instead applies hard-coded 18-damage bursts, radius 60, with all damage multipliers set to 1 against strike craft.
    - Acquisition reaches 396 units through the 10% buffer. Splash reaches fighter centers 71 units from each burst (`60 + size 11`); there is no falloff or target-count limit.
    - Fighters have **60 shield + 40 armor + 70 hull**, with armor rating 5 (B:313–321), not just 130 combined shield/hull. Replaying the damage arithmetic from S:1293–1334 gives **11 full 18-damage bursts** to kill a fresh fighter without regeneration.
    - Nominal full-overlap raw damage is `2 × 18 / 0.15 = 240 per second per corvette`, **per affected fighter**. With synchronized pairs of hits, one corvette needs six volleys, about **0.75 seconds from first impact**; two overlapping corvettes need three volleys, about **0.30 seconds**. Travel time, misses, tick quantization, and regeneration change actual results. The reported half-second wipe is mechanically plausible.
    - Formation slot spacing is 28 units laterally (S:372–375), much smaller than the splash footprint. Wing cohesion multiplies flak effectiveness.
  - **[Steering] Both attached and unattached corvettes can converge.**
    - Unattached: S:810–833 always requests 75% maximum forward speed with nominal heading offset 72 degrees. Its inward component is `105 × cos(72°) ≈ 32.45 units/second`; this is a shrinking spiral, not an orbit at a controlled radius. Index offsets make some ships even more radial. Separation considers friendly corvettes only.
    - Attached: S:648–687 drives corvettes toward slots **260 units ahead** of their flagship. At 600-unit battleship separation, two opposing two-corvette screens have only `600 − 2 × 260 × cos(0.225) ≈ 93` units of longitudinal separation. Continued flagship closure pushes screens together.
    - B:25, 242 attaches every corvette to the first battleship only. `test_3cr_3cv` contains no battleship, so its supposedly screening corvettes actually execute the unattached branch.
  - **[Broadside] Battleship maneuver code exists, but it is unstable and geometrically mismatched.**
    - S:724–748 already attempts broadside movement when the spinal is cooling down. It is incorrect to describe this as entirely missing.
    - The side choice is effectively `sign(angleToTarget − heading)`. After a small positive turn the relative angle becomes negative, so the next update requests the opposite side. Near a stationary bow-on target this can oscillate around zero instead of ever reaching a side arc.
    - The requested broadside offset is 86.4 degrees. A round trip at 0.75 rad/s takes **4.02 seconds**, longer than the 3.5-second spinal cooldown and the **2.7-second window** before `spinalReady` activates at 0.8 seconds remaining.
    - S:733–742 adds fleet spread to hull heading. With three battleships the outside ships use ±0.22 rad (12.6 degrees), beyond the spinal's ±7.2-degree arc (B:57). A ready but permanently out-of-arc spinal can keep the ship stuck in the bow-acquisition branch.
    - Port/starboard arcs are ±39.6–140.4 degrees (B:80–118). A centered forward enemy is invalid for both mounts, while S:168 initially assigns that same main target to both.
  - **[Weapon geometry] Range mismatch can produce visible cooldowns with no beam damage.**
    - Battleship target distance is 600 (S:727); side lasers have range 580 (B:94, 118). The exact check uses mount position, but the offset length is only about 17.9: at exactly 600-unit center distance even the best possible mount distance is about 582.1, still outside range. Mount offsets also matter when selecting the corrected standoff margin.
    - S:230–235 accepts center distance up to 638; S:1109–1110 consumes cooldown and records a shot; S:1138–1144 then rejects beam damage beyond the mount's actual 580-unit range. Acquisition, firing, and damage disagree.
    - S:171 infers capital targeting from `range > 800`. Increasing laser range above 800 without changing this rule will make every long-range laser hard-prefer capitals, undermining independent secondary targeting.
  - **[Cruiser doctrine] The current cruiser has no separate side batteries.**
    - B:160–208 gives it one beam turret covering ±126 degrees and one torpedo tube covering ±30 degrees. The beam can engage forward or abeam. Cruiser changes should exploit those existing mounts rather than silently add battleship-style batteries.
    - S:766–809 expressly holds its bow on target and drives toward a 480-unit slot. A lone cruiser reaches a radial equilibrium with no sustained tangential motion. The bow-alignment gate at S:919–929 suppresses translation as error rises from 12 to 24 degrees, so broadside movement cannot just be grafted onto this controller unchanged.
  - **[Threat avoidance] Escort steering does not consider spinal lanes.**
    - Neither corvette branch evaluates hostile firing arcs or rail projectiles. Fighter context steering at S:547–581 only predicts hostile missiles, not flak coverage, rails, or dangerous capital contact.
    - Physical projectiles hit the first hostile hull intersected (S:1194–1214), regardless of `targetId`. Capital-only target preference therefore cannot protect an escort that crosses a railgun's path. Friendly hulls are skipped, so friendly-fire damage is not currently a mechanic.
    - A fresh corvette is not necessarily one-shot: against a rail hit, 180 shields consume 112.5 of 180 raw damage; the remaining 67.5 causes about 41.4 armor damage, leaving hull intact. An apparent instant kill can involve prior damage, overlapping shots, or contact; capture damage provenance before changing rail lethality.
  - **[Endgame] Static equilibria and shield regeneration can hide the actual stalemate.**
    - Battleships can remain bow-on while waiting for an unreachable shot; cruisers deliberately stop at their slots. Existing deadlock handling (S:869–904) activates only for nearby capital contact, not for a static exchange at 480–600 units.
    - Battleships regenerate 40 shield/second with default zero recharge delay (B:44; S:1283–1291). A single broadside delivers only `50 × 0.5 / 1.2 ≈ 20.83 shield DPS`, below that regeneration. Its rail delivers about 82.29 shield DPS before misses. A maneuver that sacrifices rail uptime can turn a duel into a shield stalemate even if lasers now fire.
    - Deterministic outcomes are appropriate for an auto-battler. The goal is meaningful positional/damage differences, not artificial randomness or guaranteeing the initially weaker ship a win.

- **2. Correctness prerequisites for trustworthy AI and balancing**
  - **[Time integration] Use fixed simulation steps before comparing damage or steering.**
    - `frontend/src/canvas/TacticalCanvas.tsx:44–51` passes variable `min(rawDt, 0.1) × speed` directly to `update`. Fast playback changes integration granularity. `App.tsx:111–113` advances 1/30 second for a manual step.
    - Introduce a 1/60-second simulation accumulator; playback speed changes accumulated simulated time, not the integration step. Manual stepping consumes two fixed ticks. Retain unprocessed accumulator time if a per-render work cap is needed, rather than silently dropping combat time.
    - S:1077–1079 decrements cooldown inside aim. Move timer advancement into a once-per-tick phase before wing decisions; acquisition, aim, and rejection must never decrement a timer twice or stop its progress.
  - **[Contact] Replace per-update ramming damage with impact plus time-based sustained contact.**
    - S:1004–1013 applies at least 30 shield damage per overlapping pair per update, without `dt`; that corresponds to 1,800 shield damage/second at 60 Hz or 900 at 30 Hz if overlap persists. Hull contact uses an additional ×1.5 multiplier. This can masquerade as weapon damage.
    - Compute pre-resolution closing speed `c = max(0, −dot(vA − vB, normal))`. Apply one impact event on contact entry, then optional low continuous damage `contactDPS × dt`; use pair-contact state and an exit margin to prevent repeated entry damage from collision jitter. Start the continuous experiment at 5 damage/second, not 30 damage/update.
    - Resolve penetration by bounded geometric projection with mass weighting. The existing `penetration × 120 × dt` correction can overshoot at large `dt`, and exact coincident centers are skipped at S:981; supply a stable fallback normal.
    - Preserve deliberate fighter-versus-capital collision lethality initially, but make fighter steering avoid that collision envelope. Route all deaths through one death-reporting path so rams update losses, callbacks, and damage-source metrics exactly once.
  - **[Projectile correctness] Resolve swept paths and preserve weapon metadata.**
    - S:1183–1214 tests only the new projectile point. At 1/30 second a rail moves 46.7 units and flak moves 50; small targets and proximity envelopes can be skipped. Larger playback steps amplify tunneling.
    - For a moving ship use relative segment `r0 = projectileOld − shipOld`, `r1 = projectileNew − shipNew`. Solve `|r0 + u(r1−r0)|² = hitRadius²` for the earliest `u ∈ [0,1]`; choose the earliest hostile collision, rather than array order. Use the same swept geometry for flak proximity and missile interception. Integrate only the remaining lifetime portion on an expiring tick.
    - S:1198–1209 reconstructs generic weapon multipliers. Cruiser torpedoes lose their configured hull multiplier 1.8 and use 1.0; fighter plasma armor uses 0.75 instead of 1.1. Carry an immutable shot-time damage profile from the actual weapon in `ProjectileState`, and use it at impact. Rebaseline after this fix: it is a material buff to current torpedoes and plasma versus exposed defenses.
    - Move flak projectile speed/lifetime, trigger/blast radii, damage, and anti-missile damage into the weapon definition or its small optional flak profile. Set actual flak projectile speed to 1500 in B so lead calculation sees the physical weapon. The current builder value 0 causes the lead stage to skip flak entirely.
    - Skip dead ships and destroyed missiles in every later phase. The tick's `aliveShips` array is a snapshot of references, and beam damage can invalidate them mid-loop; dead hulls can currently absorb projectiles even though `applyDamage` refuses their damage. Only count interception when missile HP crosses from positive to nonpositive.
  - **[Update ordering / RISK] Separate decisions from mutation where symmetry matters.**
    - Current S:156–238 moves and fires one ship at a time; later ships see some already-moved enemies, some unmoved enemies, and damage from earlier beams. `App.tsx` inserts all blue ships before red ships.
    - Proposed tick order: advance timers/regen → capture decision positions and velocities → update wing/ship intents → integrate all movement → resolve contact → acquire/aim mounts from consistent post-movement geometry → collect eligible shots → resolve fire/projectiles → record deaths/victory.
    - Define simultaneous-shot behavior explicitly: ships alive at the common firing instant may both shoot even if one dies to the other's simultaneous beam. This is a bounded loop refactor, but changes tied engagements; verify mirrored fixtures rather than assuming the current winner is the correct oracle.

- **3. Fighter state machine, dogfight acquisition, and attack passes**
  - **[State ownership] Separate wing mission state from individual interruption state.**
    - Keep wing phases `staging → attack → egress → rejoin`. Keep wing `targetId` as the capital/strike objective, not the target every gun must use.
    - Add individual `intercept` and `recover` states plus `dogfightTargetId`, `lastThreatTime`, and a state-entry timestamp. Use a wing-only phase type so the wing coordinator cannot accidentally enter a member-only state. Apply priority `recover > intercept > wing intent`.
    - Move the current emergency-cover override into this explicit member-state decision. S:449–454 only propagates wing phase on wing transitions; a recovered individual can otherwise remain in `rejoin` until a later transition. Recompute member effective phase each tick rather than depending on the last broadcast assignment.
    - `recover` enters at the current shield/hull thresholds initially. Exit shield-driven recovery above 30 shield; for hull below 40%, permit re-engagement after a 4-second retreat when no safe anchor remains. Mark that hull-driven retreat as completed until new hull damage occurs, so the unchanged low hull does not immediately trigger it again. Hull never repairs, so a hull-only permanent retreat can create an unfinishable fighter endgame.
  - **[Interception] Interrupt formation before the enemy enters the gun envelope.**
    - Search hostile fighters within 600 units of the individual, or fighters with closest approach within 120 units in the next 1.5 seconds. For relative position `r` and velocity `v`, compute `tCPA = clamp(−dot(r,v)/max(dot(v,v), ε), 0, 1.5)` and `dCPA = |r + v × tCPA|`.
    - Enter `intercept` for a threatening candidate regardless of wing launch time. Hold the chosen opponent until invalid or a better candidate exceeds its score by 20%; require 1 second without a threat or distance beyond 720 before returning to `rejoin`.
    - Score normalized terms: `S = 3 × approachThreat + 2 × gunOpportunity + woundedBonus − distance/600 − 0.75 × friendlyAssignments`. `approachThreat` and `gunOpportunity` are clamped to [0,1]; cap `woundedBonus` at 0.5. Keep assignments in a deterministic per-decision count map, with stable numeric spawn index for ties.
    - Penalize rather than forbid an already-assigned opponent; otherwise a lone surviving enemy can become unengageable. This distributes a large flight without a global assignment optimizer.
    - Steer toward an intercept point while distant. Inside roughly 160 units blend into a tangential pursuit/break turn; preserve full escape thrust at imminent collision rather than slowing nose-to-nose. Use finite turn/acceleration prediction, not instant heading changes.
  - **[Firing policy] Make phase constrain mission commitment, not self-defense.**
    - Replace the ship-wide `canFire` boolean with per-target eligibility: all living fighter phases may fire at a valid hostile fighter in their gun arc; firing at the attack-pass objective (normally a capital, with corvette fallback) is permitted during `attack`. Recovery fire is opportunistic and never overrides escape heading.
    - An `intercept` shot must not count as completion of a capital attack pass. Track `lastMissionShotTime` or its target/mission identity rather than using any `lastFiredTime` in the wing's S:434–439 completion condition.
    - Preserve the current 120-damage/3-second plasma initially so phase/steering changes can be measured independently. It is a burst weapon, not a continuous dogfight cannon; expect discrete exchanges. Changing to a rapid-fire profile later also changes armor mitigation, so equal raw DPS would not preserve lethality.
  - **[Assembly] Remove infinite staging and defensive-fire cooldown deadlocks.**
    - Consider only healthy, non-intercepting/non-recovering members eligible for a mission wave. Launch when at least 60% of these members are assembled, or 2.5 seconds after the first eligible member reaches rally; retain the 1.8-second team wave spacing. Do not launch an empty eligible set.
    - Let a ready subset depart, with stragglers joining the next pass. Remove `every(turret.cooldown <= 0.2)` as a whole-wing barrier; defensive shots must not continually prevent departure. A member can finish reloading during its approach.
    - Reacquire dead mission targets without resetting every interceptor to staging. If no capitals exist but enemy fighters remain, the wing supports fighter skirmishing rather than forcing a bomber-style 385-unit rally around a fighter. If only corvettes remain, select a corvette as the attack-pass objective and permit mission fire against it; otherwise the new capital-specific firing policy would introduce an unfinishable matchup.
  - **[Safe route] Stop rallying inside the enemy PD screen.**
    - S:410 places rally only 385 units from the enemy capital. An enemy corvette 260 units ahead can be approximately 125 units from this rally, deep within its 360-unit flak envelope.
    - Choose rally along the wing corridor outside hostile PD disks expanded by 80 units. A directly aligned forward screen needs roughly `260 + 360 + 80 = 700` units of capital-relative standoff; this is a geometry-dependent result, not a new universal magic radius. Include slot extents when testing the wing's staging footprint.
    - Choose a flank ingress waypoint by minimizing integrated PD exposure `E = Σj ∫ max(0, 1 − distanceToPDj(t)/Rj) dt` over a small set of left/right corridor candidates. During attack, tolerate exposure to complete a pass; during staging/recovery, strongly avoid it.
    - Increase initial formation spacing to about 50 units laterally and 25–35 longitudinally. With reduced flak radius this retains a readable wing without placing almost everyone in every burst.
    - End an attack on mission shot completion, timeout, or a target-relative break radius: `Rbreak ≥ shieldRadiusTarget + shieldRadiusFighter + clearance + brakingDistance`. The existing 200-unit break distance remains a reasonable initial floor, but account for relative closing motion at high speed.
    - Define egress completion by distance outside the threat footprint and heading away, with the existing 1.4 seconds as minimum duration rather than the only criterion. Rejoin evaluates current rally safety as capitals and screens move.
  - **[Context steering] Make avoidance reachable.**
    - S:553–581 evaluates instant maximum-speed headings around 16 global sectors, although S:906–943 later constrains turn and acceleration. Evaluate each candidate after applying those same constraints over a 0.5–1.5-second rollout; include the current heading and desired heading in addition to sampled directions.
    - Add flak coverage and capital collision-envelope costs. Weight formation interest lower than imminent danger. Avoid suppressing all routes through PD, which would turn attack craft into permanent spectators.

- **4. Corvette skirmish/orbit controller and escort stationing**
  - **[Radial control] Use an annular velocity field with a stable orbit side.**
    - Let `r = target.pos − ship.pos`, `d = |r|`, `n = r/d` point toward the target, and `t = σ × (−n.y, n.x)` be the tangent. Choose `σ ∈ {−1,+1}` once per engagement from team-local slot/obstacle geometry; hold it for at least 2 seconds. It must not depend on the instantaneous sign of heading error.
    - For reciprocal mirrored opponents, use the same world-space orbit chirality by default, rather than flipping it by team color: their target-relative normals are already opposite. Opposite chirality can make both ships translate in parallel instead of orbiting each other. At zero distance use the stable separation fallback normal.
    - Start with corvette desired radius `R = 300`, radial deadband `b = 25`, and `e = sign(d−R) × max(|d−R|−b, 0)`.
    - Let `vr = clamp(0.7 × e, −0.65 × vmax, 0.70 × vmax)` and `vt = 0.55 × vmax × clamp(1 − |d−R|/300, 0.25, 1)`.
    - Desired world velocity is `vDesired = target.velocity + vr × n + vt × t + separation + threatAvoidance`, followed by speed/acceleration limits. At the ring, radial closure goes to zero while tangential movement remains; inside it, radial thrust is outward.
    - Feed this into the existing corvette lateral-thruster path at S:932–943 for both attached and unattached ships. Point the hull along feasible travel or a stable combat bearing; 360-degree PD does not require driving the bow into the victim.
  - **[Collision prevention] Avoid both teams before contact.**
    - Define shield collision radius `ri = sizei × (shieldCurrent > 0 ? 1.4 : 1)`. Pair minimum distance is `ri + rj + 30`, with an extra dynamic margin `vClosing²/(2 × aRelative)` and a reaction-step margin for rapidly closing pairs.
    - Sum local separation for nearby friendly and hostile hulls, not just friendly corvettes. Give imminent contact and spinal escape higher priority than ring/slot attraction. If weighted vectors cancel, select the safest reachable lateral candidate instead of accepting zero velocity inside danger.
    - Respect actual turn radius if using forward flight: at 105 units/second and 2 rad/s it is about 52.5 units. The proposed 300-unit ring is feasible; a last-second 20-unit avoidance turn is not.
  - **[Escort mode] Preserve protection without symmetric prow piling.**
    - Assign corvettes to the least-loaded living friendly capital, preferring the existing flagship and falling back to cruisers when no battleship survives. Reassign only on anchor death or sustained leash violation; B:242's battleship-only association is insufficient for P:73–80.
    - Use shoulder stations roughly `anchor.pos + 120 × anchor.forward ± 150 × anchor.right` for the first two escorts; stagger additional escorts into nearby safe shoulder positions. Calculate formation slots from stable indices rather than reindexing all ships on every death.
    - Blend station-keeping with threat-facing tangential patrol. Limit normal interception excursions to about 380 units from the anchor; do not chase an enemy fighter indefinitely. Apply collision/lane safety after station attraction so the slot cannot pull an escort into a rail corridor.
    - With no friendly capital, switch to the annular skirmish controller. Once a corvette is temporarily forced off its ring, use hysteresis to resume it instead of alternating orbit sides every tick.
  - **[Acceptance]** In 3v3 corvettes, require a finite standoff band and sustained angular motion, no hostile pair remaining inside the collision envelope for more than 0.5 seconds absent an unavoidable high-speed impact, and no permanent stationary clinch. Test both unattached corvettes and 1-battleship/3-corvette screens.

- **5. Independent turret acquisition, arc geometry, and interception**
  - **[Responsibilities] Keep three distinct decisions.**
    - Navigation selects an engagement objective and desired pose. A mount selects its own target from compatible hostile ships/missiles. Fire control tests current geometry, tracking, cooldown, and fighter mission policy. One nearest-enemy result cannot serve all three roles.
    - Reuse `TurretMountState.currentTargetId` (T:33), currently initialized but never maintained by the simulation. Keep target kind separately when the candidate can be a missile; an explicit discriminated target reference avoids ambiguous ship/projectile handling.
  - **[Geometry] Filter mount-local candidates before scoring.**
    - For each mount compute `m = ship.pos + rotate(offset, heading)` and candidate aim point `q`. Local bearing is `a = wrap(atan2(q.y−m.y, q.x−m.x) − heading)`.
    - Use a single shared arc predicate for acquisition and aiming. For current non-wrapping arcs, `minArc ≤ a ≤ maxArc`; explicitly treat `[-π,π]` as full-circle. If wrapped arcs are later used, compare wrapped distance to arc center/half-width rather than clamping angles across the discontinuity.
    - Keep constrained mount rotation inside its permitted interval. In particular, a cruiser beam moving between −125 and +125 degrees must traverse its forward allowed arc, not take the wrapped shortcut through the prohibited rear sector; reserve unrestricted shortest-angle rotation for full-circle mounts.
    - Beam feasibility is `distance(m, target.pos) ≤ range` and actual target bearing inside the arc. Projectile feasibility checks present acquisition distance and the predicted launch bearing; lead outside the arc is not a valid shot just because current target position is inside it.
    - Use an acquisition-lock buffer only for retaining a target, not extending legal firing range. A retained out-of-range lock cannot consume cooldown. Commit cooldown and `lastFiredTime` only when a beam or projectile is genuinely emitted.
    - Re-evaluate residual alignment **after** advancing `currentLocalAngle`; S:1099 currently tests the pre-step error. Preserve tolerances initially (spinal 0.08 rad, other 0.15 rad), then measure small-target misses before tightening them.
  - **[Policy] Replace the `range > 800` heuristic with explicit doctrine.**
    - Spinal: prefer viable capital targets in the spinal arc; if none can be shot, expose an aim request to navigation. The fallback may engage an escort when no viable capital shot exists, but do not discard all in-arc choices because a distant off-arc capital is alive.
    - Torpedo: prefer capital hulls compatible with its launch arc and practical intercept horizon. It is a forward projectile role, independent of range value.
    - Broadside/heavy beam: score all compatible hostile hulls in the mount arc; armor-exposed capitals and nearby escorts may outrank a forward objective. Opposite mounts can select different enemies in the same tick.
    - PD: rank incoming missiles threatening self/anchor by time-to-impact, then strike craft, then other hulls. S:310–321 currently selects any nearest hostile missile, including one moving away; use closest approach and positive HP. Apply mount arc constraints to missiles as well.
    - Fighters: local hostile fighters can override mission targets according to section 3. The wing objective remains intact for navigation and pass completion.
  - **[Scoring and stability]**
    - A starter normalized mount score is `S = 3 × roleFit + 2 × threat + finishingValue − distance/range − 0.4 × turnTime − saturationPenalty`. Define role fit/threat/finishing value on [0,1]; `turnTime = angularError/trackingSpeed` in seconds. For immediate missile threats, priority may override the weighted ship score.
    - Keep a valid current lock unless the new score exceeds it by `0.2 × max(|oldScore|, 1)`; reacquire immediately if dead, incompatible, or no longer in a reachable arc. Use a 0.2-second rescan interval with immediate invalidation, but continue aiming/range checks every tick.
    - For PD, account for shells already committed to the same missile only when their predicted paths are plausibly intercepting it; do not reserve a target forever because a missed shell still carries its ID. Against ships use a soft saturation penalty, not an invulnerability-producing hard cap.
  - **[Ballistics] Solve the actual intercept rather than `distance/projectileSpeed`.**
    - Current S:220–225 assumes lead time from present distance. Let `r = target.pos − muzzle`, `v = target.velocity`, and `s = projectileSpeed`; projectiles currently do not inherit shooter velocity, so do **not** subtract it here.
    - Solve `(v·v − s²)t² + 2(r·v)t + r·r = 0`. Use the smallest positive root within projectile lifetime; handle the near-linear coefficient case. Aim at `target.pos + v × t`. If there is no feasible intercept, select another target or defer fire instead of claiming a valid led shot.
    - PD should use missile velocity too; it currently sets `turretTarget = null`, losing the lead path. Torpedoes currently fly straight after launch despite storing a target ID (S:1170–1223); do not assume homing when evaluating longer-range torpedo accuracy.
    - Preserve the existing 1.25× range-derived projectile lifetime initially as an explicit travel allowance; acquisition remains limited to nominal range. Range retuning must update lifetime consistently. Swept collision remains necessary even at a fixed tick.
  - **[Acceptance]** With stationary enemies forward, port, and starboard inside range, the spinal and both batteries must independently engage their valid targets. An out-of-arc preferred capital must not starve an in-arc secondary mount. A target just outside beam range must leave the cooldown ready and produce no false shot event.

- **6. Capital firing/movement cycle and endgame repositioning**
  - **[State / RISK] Introduce a small explicit maneuver cycle.**
    - Suggested states: `approach → bow_acquire → broadside_hold → bow_reacquire`; `reposition` is an emergency/low-shield override. State records need entry time, stable broadside side, and engagement target identity. Use current turret cooldowns rather than a second parallel reload clock.
    - `approach`: close toward a weapon-derived engagement band while avoiding contacts. `bow_acquire`: align the prow to the actual spinal target; transition on a confirmed spinal shot, not merely timer readiness. `broadside_hold`: turn just far enough for the chosen side battery and permit independent targets. `bow_reacquire`: return early enough to meet the next firing window.
    - If bow acquisition has no viable shot for about 1.5 seconds, choose another valid objective or reposition; do not park forever with a ready spinal. Navigation must know which capital the spinal selected, since the current movement target and spinal override can disagree.
  - **[Stable side and feasible timing] Prefer a shallow presentation, not a compulsory 90-degree turn.**
    - Start with a **48-degree** hull offset, placing the main enemy about 8.4 degrees inside the side battery's inner arc. Define side unambiguously: for desired target local bearing `β = ±48°`, set hull heading `θdesired = angleToTarget − β`. Negative `β` presents port; positive presents starboard.
    - Choose side using valid battery opportunities and lane/neighbor clearance; hold it through the cycle. Reconsider on target death, obstacle, or a clear score advantage after a minimum dwell. Remove the instantaneous `relAngle` sign flip.
    - At 0.75 rad/s, a 48-degree round trip takes **2.23 seconds**, leaving about **1.27 seconds** of a 3.5-second reload before tracking/target-motion allowances. This is feasible for one battery pulse when geometry cooperates, but does not guarantee every cycle can include it.
    - Compute `tReturn = max(0, |wrap(angleToSpinalTarget − heading)| − usableSpinalHalfArc)/turnRate`, then include turret tracking and relative bearing-rate margin. Begin `bow_reacquire` when `cooldownRemaining ≤ tReturn + 0.25 seconds`; use a conservative full bow alignment estimate if the gimbal margin is uncertain.
    - Avoid broadside commitment when target angular velocity or ship turn time makes the return infeasible. Favor rail uptime over oscillatory attempts to fire every weapon on every reload. Rearrange formation through positions, not persistent bow offsets outside the gimbal.
  - **[Translation] Decouple distance control from gun-facing orientation.**
    - Give battleships a bounded desired world velocity, analogous to the existing cruiser thrusters, so they can manage radial separation while angling. Keep speeds within current 38 maximum; start lateral limit around 0.30–0.35 maximum rather than introducing agile fighter-like strafing.
    - Use the annular controller with a deadband, radial error gain around 0.35/s, and tangential speed 0.25–0.35 maximum during broadside/reposition. Constrain the combined velocity and acceleration; do not add unclamped target-velocity feed-forward to an already saturated vector.
    - Cruisers keep their forward torpedo identity: after torpedo release, use a 35–45-degree oblique transit and lateral orbit at about 0.30 maximum speed. Their ±126-degree beam remains usable. Reacquire the ±30-degree torpedo arc before reload. A symmetric 40-degree round trip at 1 rad/s needs about 1.40 seconds of their 4.5-second cooldown.
    - Replace the cruiser's unconditional 12–24-degree bow brake with a state-aware limit: strict during initial/bow acquisition, relaxed during deliberate oblique transit. Otherwise the new maneuver requests rotation while its translation is multiplied to zero.
  - **[Endgame tactics] Make repositioning responsive and bounded.**
    - Define shield ratio `h = currentShield/maxShield`; enlarge desired range smoothly by up to 80 units as `h` falls below 0.35, with a return threshold above 0.55 to avoid toggling. Stay inside the selected battery envelope for optimal-range doctrine.
    - Trigger a 2–3-second lateral/rear reposition after a failed acquisition, imminent collision, or unfavorable low-shield exchange; reassess and re-enter rather than kiting forever. Detect low meaningful relative movement over roughly 3 seconds while weapons are unusable, not only physical overlap.
    - Implement existing `GroupOrder.stance` (T:6, 100–104), currently not passed into movement: `optimal_range` uses combined-weapon bands; `max_range` intentionally prioritizes long-range weapons and may forgo beams; `brawl` closes to a shorter but collision-safe band. `screen_and_defend` should select anchor-oriented defense rather than silently acting as nearest-target behavior.
    - Exact mirror fleets may still trade symmetrically. Evaluate motion, weapon utilization, and outcome symmetry under team swapping; do not inject randomness to manufacture drama.

- **7. Escort spinal danger-corridor avoidance**
  - **[Geometry] Predict an expanded finite firing corridor from each hostile spinal mount.**
    - At prediction time `τ`, let muzzle `m(τ) = enemy.pos + enemy.velocity × τ + rotate(offset, predictedHeading)`, forward unit `f`, lateral unit `g`, and candidate escort position `p(τ)`.
    - Set `x = dot(p−m, f)`, `y = dot(p−m, g)`. A threatening finite cone requires `0 < x < weaponRange + escortRadius` and `|y| < w(x)`, where `w(x) = escortRadius + safetyMargin + x × tan(halfArc + headingUncertainty)`.
    - For the current spinal, `halfArc = π/25 = 7.2°`. At x = 600, the gimbal envelope alone is about 75.8 units wide on either side before escort radius/margin. Evaluate both the currently aimed barrel line and the prospective gimbal envelope; they represent different risks.
    - Bound heading uncertainty using actual enemy turn rate and plausible aim toward its navigation target. Do not dilate all future headings into a permanent 360-degree exclusion zone.
  - **[Timing] Threat depends on readiness, flight time, and actual shells.**
    - For a candidate crossing at longitudinal distance x, estimate danger arrival `tHit ≈ max(cooldownRemaining, tAim) + x/projectileSpeed`; initial horizon 1.5 seconds. At 600 units rail flight alone is about 0.43 seconds, so reacting only to a visible shell is often late.
    - Score `Dcone = readinessWeight × proximityToCenter × rangeWeight`, with readiness highest when firing can occur within the rollout. Predict both cooldown and enemy turn feasibility; maintain a lower residual cost during reload rather than treating cooldown as absolute safety.
    - Separately compute closest approach to already-fired rail projectiles using relative velocity or swept rollout. A gun on cooldown can have a lethal shell in flight. Exclude missiles/rails already destroyed or expired.
  - **[Steering] Escape laterally, then resume the screen.**
    - Evaluate a small reachable velocity set: current intent, left/right lateral escape, brake, and retreat. Integrate with the same thrust/turn limits for 0.5–1.5 seconds; sample cone clearance along the path, not only the endpoint.
    - Prefer a candidate that clears the nearest cone edge: desired side is `sign(y)`, with a stable slot-based tie-break when centered. Penalize paths crossing the cone center even if their endpoint looks safe. Hold escape side for about 1 second to prevent indecision.
    - Use danger priority `imminent impact/contact > spinal corridor > screen leash > orbit/slot preference`. A corvette must be allowed to temporarily violate its station to survive.
    - Reuse the geometric helpers with fighter context steering where relevant, but keep escort and fighter tactical weights separate. No global path planner or new physics engine is needed for these local avoidance rules.
  - **[Acceptance]** A fresh corvette approaching a ready hostile battleship from the front should visibly select a shoulder route before entering the imminent firing corridor when a reachable safe route exists. Repeat while the spinal is reloading, with an in-flight rail, and while the assigned flagship is moving; distinguish feasible avoidance from an already-unavoidable initial condition.

- **8. Initial balance proposal and its dependencies**
  - **[Range] Increase separation in proportion to the actual encounter geometry.**
    - The active app spawns capital centers at ±580, only **1,160 units apart** (`App.tsx:75–91`). There is no finite arena clamp in S:636–952. Screen pixels/zoom are not world-map dimensions. The current 980 rail and 1,100 torpedo are already comparable to the deployment gap; short preferred movement distances are a major reason fleets close.
    - Proposed initial weapon ranges in B: spinal rail **980 → 1,350**; cruiser torpedo **1,100 → 1,450**; battleship side batteries **580 → 950**; cruiser heavy beam **580 → 900**. Keep fighter plasma **480** and PD **360** initially.
    - Proposed center-distance bands: battleship optimal **800–880**, nominal **840**; cruiser optimal **700–780**, nominal **740**; independent corvette **275–325**, nominal **300**. Suggested max-range centers: battleship **1,180**, cruiser **1,250**; brawl centers roughly **620** and **560**, subject to collision safety.
    - Derive feasibility from mounts: conservative center distance for a battery is `Rcenter ≤ weaponRange − |mountOffset| − motionMargin`. A battleship at 840 with offset length about 17.9 and margin 50 is safely inside 950. Bound dynamic low-shield range increases by this envelope rather than allowing nominal optimum to exceed useful reach again.
    - With ±580 deployment, the proposed capital weapons can engage immediately. If an opening approach interval is desired, pair the range experiment with deployment at **±800** (1,600-unit center gap), updating the source spawn constants in App and inspecting camera framing. Treat these as explicit paired scenario settings when comparing results, not an unnoticed consequence of a range buff.
    - Longer straight-flying torpedoes have more flight/PD exposure: 1,450/320 ≈ 4.53 seconds of nominal flight, and current 1.25 lifetime allowance yields about 5.66 seconds. Correct intercepts and projectile metadata before deciding they need more speed or damage.
  - **[Flak] Preserve anti-missile defense while reducing swarm-wide instant damage.**
    - Start both mounts at **10 ship damage**, **0.30-second cooldown**, **34-unit blast radius**, **18-unit proximity trigger**, and **1,500 projectile speed**. Keep 360 acquisition range without the 10% firing extension. Use an explicit lifetime near 0.32 seconds to cover travel and modest lead, then validate swept fuse behavior against near-range-limit targets.
    - Use separate **18 anti-missile damage**, preserving two full hits to destroy current 25-HP missiles. Preserve current low non-strike multipliers initially: shield 0.15, armor 0.1, hull 0.15. Make these the actual configured values, rather than keeping misleading builder multipliers.
    - For ships, surface distance `s = max(0, |ship.pos − burst.pos| − ship.size)` gives full damage for `s ≤ 18`; otherwise use `falloff = clamp((34−s)/(34−18), 0, 1)`. The full-damage core matches the 18-unit proximity trigger so a direct swept-fuse intercept receives the stated damage. Damage is zero outside 34 surface-distance units. Use the separate anti-missile profile without ship falloff initially, then measure interception rates.
    - At full overlap the new dual-mount ceiling is `2 × 10 / 0.30 ≈ 66.7 raw DPS`, about 72% below current. Replaying current fighter armor math requires **20 full 10-damage hits**: ten paired volleys, **2.7 seconds after first impact** from one corvette, or roughly **1.2 seconds** from two perfectly synchronized corvettes. Falloff and spread increase survival; focus fire remains dangerous.
    - Phase the second mount initially by half its cooldown (0.15 seconds) to avoid universal frame-zero paired salvos. This smooths damage but is not a substitute for the damage/radius correction. Recalculate observed TTK with that phasing rather than quoting the synchronized lower bounds as playtest measurements.
    - Shrinking radius also reduces multi-missile interception even with preserved per-hit damage; measure that explicitly before further nerfing tracking or fire rate. Do not assume preserving missile damage preserves overall defense strength.
    - Update `TacticalCanvas.tsx:32–34`, which hard-codes visual burst radius 60, to consume the actual event/profile radius so the visual footprint matches gameplay.
  - **[Capital endurance] Tune recharge only after firing utilization is fixed.**
    - Start battleship `shieldRechargeDelay` at **1.5 seconds** and retain regeneration **40/second**; keep cruiser 1.2, corvette 2.5, fighter 3.5 initially. With 1.2-second battery cadence, sustained laser hits suppress regeneration rather than losing the entire exchange to passive recharge.
    - This changes the value of cover and every small hit, including weak flak. Compare the 1.5-second-delay case against the original zero-delay case; if incidental flak suppresses battleship recharge too completely, decide explicitly whether a minimum effective-damage threshold should reset capital recharge. Do not add that rule without observing the problem.
    - Keep rail/beam/torpedo base damage and existing hull pools initially. Metadata correction already raises torpedo hull and plasma armor effectiveness; adding damage buffs at the same time would obscure the cause of improved kills.
  - **[Preset accuracy] Use real compositions and equal-budget comparisons.**
    - P:57–62 describes 12 fighters as four wings, but S:336–340 creates `max(1, ceil(12/6), round(12/5)) = 2` wings of six. Correct the description or explicitly change formation sizing as a separately measured choice.
    - P's `combined` is 4,620 credits (1 BB + 2 CV + 6 SC); the default blue fleet in App is 4,380 (1 BB + 2 CV + 4 SC). App initially selects missing ID `swarm` and falls back to `combined`. The initial scene is not a mirrored-fleet fairness test.
    - Preserve unequal-budget 3-ship presets as controller diagnostics, not balance verdicts. Add/construct equal-budget or explicitly normalized scenarios for doctrine comparisons. Verify the cruiser/corvette flotilla actually assigns escorts after anchor fallback is implemented.

- **10. Architectural boundaries and performance budget**
  - **[Separation of concerns]** Keep `fleetBuilder.ts` authoritative for ship/weapon definitions; keep `fleetPresets.ts` descriptive and compositional. Do not hide damage overrides in projectile branches or encode target doctrine in weapon range. Preserve existing builder-based fixtures so production and test fleets exercise the same definitions.
  - **[Proportional structure]** The current 1,358-line simulation owns wing strategy, movement, targeting, collision, damage, and telemetry. Extract only cohesive helpers needed by this change: mount geometry/intercepts, local maneuver/threat scoring, and explicit state transitions. Avoid replacing the entire engine or introducing inheritance hierarchies for four hull types.
  - **[Cost]** Independent acquisition is initially `O(mounts × enemies)`; acceptable for the current small presets, especially at 5 Hz scans. Build living ships by ID/team once per decision phase instead of filtering the entire fleet inside each mount/wing repeatedly. Use stable numeric spawn indices for local ties, not lexicographic IDs where `sc_10` precedes `sc_2`.
  - **[Hot paths]** Fighter sector scoring already scans projectiles and peers per candidate (S:553–601); filter nearby threats once per craft before rolling candidates. Cruiser clearance may run at least 32 pair passes (S:1043), so measure it before adding a second general solver. Add spatial indexing only if profiling at 30+ fighters shows a real bottleneck.
  - **[Priority summary]** The highest-leverage fixes are the fighter fire gate and wing-target override, truthful flak damage/falloff, independent arc-valid mount acquisition, stable shallow capital turns, and distance-aware corvette velocity. Fixed-step/contact/projectile correctness is the prerequisite that makes their balance results trustworthy.
